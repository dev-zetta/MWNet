# Linux 1.0.0 preparation and evidence reuse

The maintainer authorized preparing, automatically validating, deploying and publishing Linux stable 1.0.0 on November 5, 2026. The selected baseline is the published Linux beta runtime `adc751899eade2b04086a794753f9540124c5e05`. Its package/rendered acceptance, [completed independent eight-hour synthetic workload](2026-11-05-nuc-acceptance.md), [verified live NUC deployment](2026-11-05-beta-deployment.md) and [public download verification](2026-11-05-beta-release.md) remain preserved with their original source and binary identities.

The production source change for this candidate is limited to `MWNET_VERSION`, from `1.0.0-beta.1` to `1.0.0`; gameplay protocol 13, cryptographic labels, game/server/directory algorithms, dependencies, content and persistence formats are unchanged. Later headless test-harness corrections remain QA changes and are not relabeled as production changes. The Linux packager now emits `RELEASE.txt` and stable manifest scope for a numeric release version, retaining preview instructions for prereleases. Seven archive/notice tests passed, including actual stable and prerelease archive inventory checks and all sixteen required notice inputs.

Under the recorded release policy, the completed beta overnight evidence is reusable for this version/packaging-only transition after the final source/compiler-input comparison confirms that scope. The original eight-hour receipt is not a new run of stable binaries, and its hashes will not be replaced. A production, dependency, configuration or resource-lifetime change discovered during preparation requires reassessment and relevant validation before publication. Stable binaries, package/control/rendered checks, source/notices, deployment and public download verification receive their own identities and receipts. Publication remains pending at this checkpoint.

Builds reuse the existing Ubuntu 24.04 builder image and a separate copied cache on validated disk, with bounded concurrency and memory. The original beta cache, packages, frozen acceptance inputs, failed runs and evidence remain unchanged. No manual gameplay is requested. Windows/macOS distribution and graphical duties remain outside this Linux-only promotion, and no populated sixteen-player capacity claim is added. The NUC crash diagnostics remain enabled; its earlier freezes still have no established cause.
